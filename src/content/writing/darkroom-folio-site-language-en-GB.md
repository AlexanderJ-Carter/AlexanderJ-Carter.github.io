Personal sites often swing between two extremes: SaaS dashboard or blank Notion page. In the 2026 redesign I chose a middle path — **Darkroom Folio**: a darkroom / print-room metaphor that unifies photography, writing, and utility pages under one visual grammar.

The question is not “which theme colour?” but **“does every page feel like the same editorial room?”**

## Three anchors: Expose / Focus / Print

The homepage “darkroom principles” are a decision order for the whole site:

1. **Expose** — First screen: one thing only (a real photograph or one thesis).
2. **Focus** — Each block does one job; folio marks (`01 · Studio`) replace nested card stacks.
3. **Print** — Details converge at “print time”: type scale, borders, film-edge dividers — not first-screen motion.

Before shipping a page I ask which stage it belongs to. If it tries to do all three, the IA usually isn’t tight enough.

## Tokens: cyanotype paper, not inverted light mode

Backgrounds echo cyanotype paper: cool neutrals with a chemical hint. Accent colour is for actions only. Dark mode gets its own steps — not a simple invert. Film-edge lines act as section punctuation instead of divider spam.

## folio-mark: navigation like turning folio pages

Small monospace marks locate the reader (`Dossier · AS`, `02 · Person`). On About, personal dossier slots stay intentionally blank — “Reserved” beats lorem ipsum for honesty and maintenance.

## Boundaries with utility pages

Tool pages (Now, currency, QR) may feel more “panel-like” but share PageHero and chrome. The `/next` lab can be bolder, gated behind its own door.

## Summary

Darkroom Folio turns a page collection into a folio still in the wash. Expose / Focus / Print is the workflow; tokens and marks are the grammar; empty slots and feature flags keep the system honest as it grows.
