Many people post "currently reading" on social feeds; a few days later it is gone. I wanted one page that could grow slowly: clear routes, findable notes, permission to remove books that expired.

`/reading-list` on alexander.xin exists for that. It is not a bestseller chart or affiliate funnel — a map for future me.

## What a Reading List Page Solves

1. **Fewer repeated mistakes**: same topic — what I read, why I dropped it — written down so next time I hesitate less.
2. **Recommendations you can verify**: when someone asks what to read for web work, point to an ordered route, not three bestsellers from memory.
3. **Reading flows back into making**: photography books, engineering books, essays should return to writing and gallery, not die in highlighted sentences.

A page of titles and stars differs little from a shopping cart. Value is **why this book sits on this route, and what changed after finishing.**

## Structure: Route > Entry > Reflection

Three layers:

- **Route**: e.g. "static site engineering", "reading light", "long-form writing". Each route has a paragraph on start and end.
- **Entry**: title, author, status (reading / finished / paused).
- **Reflection**: short paragraph or several; incomplete allowed, must be specific.

In reflections I force one actionable line: e.g. "after reading I removed the stats strip from the homepage first screen" — not "very inspiring".

## Deleting Is Prerequisite for Trust

Pausing or removing from the public list matters like deleting photos in culling. Books stuck on "plan to read" for three years poison trust. Better a short list than performed erudition.

Seasonal review: which routes still active, which book replaced by new understanding. Personal sites may be slow; they should be honest.

## Cross-links to Writing and Projects

Ideal loop:

- Read performance book → write performance practice → change site build;
- Read photography book → tighten culling → update gallery notes;
- Read design book → tighten palette and type scale.

Reading list becomes index, not island. Book list without creative outlet slowly becomes museum labels.

## Keep Implementation Boring

Markdown or structured data — what matters is Git, multilingual capability, static generation. Do not add login-heavy "reading social" apps. This site picks maintainable data shapes and spends time reading.

## Building Routes Without Pretending Expertise

A route is a hypothesis: "if I read these in this order, I will understand X better." It can be wrong. Updating the route when a book fails you is stronger than leaving a shrine to abandoned ambition.

I also note **anti-routes** sometimes: books I do not recommend for a given goal, and why. That saves visitors time and signals you actually read, not only collected spines.

## Public vs Private Notes

Not every margin scribble belongs online. Public reflections should be distilled — one concrete change, one honest limitation ("useful for theory, weak on practice"). Private notes can stay in whatever app you use; the site page is the curated layer.

## Multilingual Reading Lists

If your site is multilingual, decide whether book titles stay in original language (often yes) while route descriptions translate. Mixing without thought looks sloppy. I keep author names and titles as published; route intros live in i18n files like other page copy.

## Status Labels That Mean Something

"In progress" should mean you opened it this month, not "I bought it in 2022". "Paused" is honest — life interrupted. "Finished" deserves the reflection paragraph. Empty status gymnastics erode the page faster than a short list.

## Seasonal Routes

Some routes are winter reading (long technical books), some are summer (lighter essay collections). Naming seasons in route intros sets expectations — visitors understand why a list skews heavy in January and photography in July.

## Linking From Articles

When a post references a book, link to the reading-list entry if one exists. That closes the loop between essay and bibliography. Otherwise the list and writing feel like parallel tracks that never meet.

## Closing Thoughts

A reading list as a site page fights feed amnesia. Routes, entries, actionable reflections, plus willingness to delete — enough. It will not make you look busier, but it makes the site feel like a brain running over time, not a decorative link wall.
