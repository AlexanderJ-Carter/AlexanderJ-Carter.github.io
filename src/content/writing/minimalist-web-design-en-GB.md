"Minimalism" is overused. People hear it and picture white backgrounds, one sans-serif, and an about page with a single sentence. That is not minimalism — that is empty.

Real minimalism is controlled richness. It looks simple because type, spacing, and colour were decided with care. It is not "doing less work" but "doing exactly enough".

## What Minimalism Is Not

**Not poverty.** One title, one paragraph, one button can still involve infinite micro-decisions — tracking, line-height, radius, distance to the button.

**Not laziness.** Text-only layouts expose weak alignment faster than decorative pages. A 1px tracking error reads loud when nothing else distracts.

**Not one visual style.** Dark backgrounds, serifs, and tight type can be minimal. The test is intent: does every element serve one goal? Is anything ornamental without purpose?

## Two Kinds of Whitespace

**Decorative whitespace** fills space to look "premium". It does not structure reading — it performs taste.

**Structural whitespace** creates hierarchy and rhythm. Gap between headline and body says "new topic starts here". Gap between column and sidebar says "boundary".

In Chinese painting, blank space is not "unpainted" — it is mist, water, infinity. Web whitespace should carry intent the same way.

## Quiet Luxury

Good minimal sites feel **calm but not boring, restrained but not cold** — digital quiet luxury.

- **Colour**: few roles, clear relationships.
- **Type**: becomes the main voice when ornament is gone — weight and scale carry hierarchy.
- **Motion**: fades and hovers that smooth flow; not particle storms on first paint.

## Minimal Genes in Chinese Aesthetics

**留白** — blank as composition. **意境** — atmosphere beyond literal content. **含蓄** — not saying everything; leaving room for the viewer.

A dark personal site with serif about copy and few lines can convey introspection, not just biography.

## Practice

1. Cap the palette — often three colours total.
2. Fix type scale before layout games.
3. Group with spacing, do not distribute evenly.
4. Before adding anything: does the page break without it?
5. Break one rule on purpose — a accent, a size bump — so calm does not become numb.

## Closing

Minimalism is a means, not an end. The visitor should feel not "there is nothing here" but "everything is where it belongs". Less is more — but **more** is the point.
