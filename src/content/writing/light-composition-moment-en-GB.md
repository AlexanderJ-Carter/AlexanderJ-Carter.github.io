Burst mode and cheap storage made shooting cheap and keeping hard. After import, selecting hurts more than the shutter: straight composition but flat light; beautiful light but empty story; story present but blurred on the decisive frame.

I filter with a short list: **light, composition, moment.** Not original theory — three questions that hold up in practice. Gallery images on alexander.xin usually score at least two "yes", ideally three.

## Light: Is There an Emotional Floor?

Do not ask "bright enough" first — ask whether this light has character.

- Side window light gives objects volume;
- Back light pulls end-of-day feeling;
- Overcast soft light is honest on skin and paper;
- Hard shadow turns pavement into graphics.

If a photo stands in monochrome, light structure is probably right. If only filter vibe, no direction or quality, retouching feels like skin.

In culling I down-rank obvious "flat selfie light" — unless moment is so strong flat light does not matter.

## Composition: Does the Eye Know Where to Go?

Composition is not a rule-of-thirds certificate but:

- Subject clear;
- Distraction cropped or subdued;
- Lines / gaze guidance works;
- No half stranger at the edge stealing attention (unless chaos is the point).

I ask: squint at the thumbnail — still recognise the subject? Thumbnail failures rarely become large-print saves unless texture-level appreciation is the goal.

"Almost" composition can be cropped; crop cannot fill empty content, only rearrange it.

## Moment: Human Trace?

Landscape may lack people but not timing: cloud split for a second, water just stilled, pedestrian exiting frame. Portraits and street work show it clearer — gaze, gesture, breath before smile.

In a burst of three, often only one frame has relaxed shoulders. Cull for that frame, not the technically perfect one.

Without moment, the image is a set dressed but performance not started. Fine for practice; not urgent for gallery.

## Practice: From 200 Frames to 5

1. **Round one fast**: mark possible vs no; no editing. Drop blur, exposure failures, exact duplicates.
2. **Round two three questions**: light / composition / moment 0–2 each; low total out.
3. **Round three sequence**: one strongest per scene unless story needs dialogue of two.
4. **Round four overnight**: next day, what still liked gets wall or homepage feature.

Homepage feature is harsher than album — it is site character. Better sparse than diluted.

## Relation to the Technical Site

Photo culling resembles code review: resist "I shot/wrote it so it must stay". Deletion is skill. Storage is cheap; attention is not; site bandwidth and visitor time are not either.

Gallery captions stay short because images should stand. If three hundred words are needed to make it interesting, it may not meet "stay" threshold — fine in an article as process, not gallery as conclusion.

## Why "Fine" Is Most Dangerous

Hardest is not obvious rejects but "fine": exposure OK, roughly level, subject barely holds. They fill drives and hesitation.

Default for "fine": archive folder, not gallery, not homepage. If you still remember one three months later, fish it out and re-evaluate. Memory is a filter — forgotten usually means not worth showing strangers.

Another trap: series obsession — thirty frames same corner, micro angle differences, keep all. Series need narrative progression (far→near, empty→person, day→night), not parameter jitter.

## Phone and Camera, Same Standard

Different tools, same three questions. Computational photography can "fix" flat light and erase character. I check originals or disable heavy beauty preview — liking the scene, not algorithm skin.

Phone advantage: always present, easier to wait for moment. Disadvantage: too easy to spray. Overnight cool-down and thumbnail test matter more.

## Rating vs Feeling

I stopped using star ratings in private culling — they imply precision the process does not have. Light / composition / moment scores are coarse on purpose. A "2" on moment with "2" on light still might stay if the pair tells a story; two "1"s rarely survive unless I am building a deliberate series about failure.

## Gallery Sequencing

Order on the page is another edit. Adjacent images should not repeat the same composition recipe unless the sequence is the point. When updating alexander.xin gallery, I reorder after culling — not only append new work to the end.

## Closing Thoughts

Photos that stay usually have light with character, readable structure, warm moment. Fail the three questions — archive generously; do not dilute the strong few. Next import: do not edit first; ask three times, then decide who earns a slot on the site.
